About the Unix Timestamp & Epoch Converter

The XESTUS Unix Timestamp & Epoch Converter is a developer utility designed to translate numeric Unix epoch timestamps into human-readable calendar dates and vice versa. The Unix epoch is defined as the number of seconds that have elapsed since 00:00:00 UTC on 1 January 1970. This tool handles both standard second timestamps and 13-digit JavaScript millisecond timestamps, supporting ISO 8601, RFC 2822, UTC, and your local timezone.

Mathematical Formulas & Technical Mechanics

Under the hood, calculations and operations strictly adhere to validated standards:

Epoch Mathematical Representation
1. Seconds to Human Date:
   Date = new Date(UnixTimestampInSeconds × 1000)

2. Human Date to Epoch Seconds:
   UnixEpochSeconds = Math.floor(DateObject.getTime() / 1000)

3. Year 2038 Problem (Y2038):
   32-bit signed integers overflow on 19 January 2038 at 03:14:07 UTC.
   Modern 64-bit timestamps prevent this overflow for billions of years.

Frequently Asked Questions

A 10-digit timestamp represents seconds elapsed since 1970 (standard Unix format in C, Python, PHP), while a 13-digit timestamp represents milliseconds (standard in JavaScript Date.now() and Java).

Calculations rely on UTC epoch seconds, which are timezone-neutral, ensuring zero distortion from local clock changes.
